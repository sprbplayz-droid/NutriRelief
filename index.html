About the Study

Why Packaged Food Deserves a Closer Look

Packaged snacks are consumed by millions of Indians every single day — from school tiffin boxes to office breaks. Yet most consumers never look past the attractive packaging to read what's actually inside. Our project sets out to change that.

We selected two of the most widely consumed snack products in India — Lay's Classic Salted Chips and Britannia Little Hearts biscuits — and subjected their ingredient lists and nutritional labels to a rigorous health analysis.

The findings are concerning. Both products contain ingredients that, when consumed regularly and in excess, are strongly linked to cardiovascular disease, obesity, liver damage, and hyperactivity in children. Our goal is not to alarm, but to inform.

Key Findings

At a Glance

🧂

Sodium Overload

A single serving of Lay's provides up to 12% of the recommended daily sodium intake — and most people eat more than one serving at a time.

🧪

Artificial Additives

Both products use flavour enhancers, artificial colours and emulsifiers that have been flagged by international food safety bodies for long-term health risks.

🫀

Refined Carbohydrates

Little Hearts' base ingredient — refined wheat flour (maida) — spikes blood sugar rapidly and contributes to insulin resistance over time.

🛢️

Harmful Oils

Both snacks are fried or baked in palm oil or partially hydrogenated oils, major contributors to raised LDL cholesterol and arterial blockage.

Our Method
01

Ingredient Collection

We recorded the full ingredient list from product packaging and cross-referenced with FSSAI and WHO ingredient databases.

02

Hazard Classification

Each ingredient was classified as Safe, Moderate Concern, or High Concern based on peer-reviewed nutritional literature.

03

Health Impact Analysis

We studied the cumulative effects of regular consumption, referencing clinical studies on sodium, saturated fats, and food additives.

04

Rating & Conclusion

Products were assigned a health rating out of 5, factoring in ingredient quality, nutritional density, and processing level.
